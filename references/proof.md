# Proof: Capabilities, Evidence, Numbers

## Capabilities and evidence

Choose two or three things the person mainly does. For each, assign evidence that a visitor can open.

- **One evidence item serves one capability.** If a project appears under two capabilities, or two projects share the same origin (one is extracted from the other), the section reads as repetition. Keep the stronger item, and mention the other as context inside it.
- **A gap is information.** If a capability has no evidence, tell the owner. Options: build a project that fills it, borrow public work done elsewhere with the right boundary, or drop the capability. Reverse-planning is allowed: "If these are the three things I want to be known for, what small project would prove the weakest one?"
- **A project that is no longer running can still be evidence** when it is labeled plainly (for example "no longer publicly running; source private") and a real artifact remains. A dead link presented as live is worse than omitting it.

## Numbers

- Use only numbers the owner can source: a resume line, a repository page, an analytics screenshot, a post's counters. Record the source and the date.
- Show the two or three that persuade most, in a row of solid tiles with a plain caption each. A number that says the same thing as another (likes and saves) can go on the detail page only.
- State the period or the scope in one short line ("Cold-start figures, from my resume").
- Round with an approximation sign when the source is approximate ("about 50,000"), and keep the exact figure only when the source is exact.
- Do not show numbers that belong to an employer, such as internal usage, headcount, or process counts, unless the owner confirms they are public. Ask. When in doubt, leave them out and describe the person's own part.

## Public reaction

Screenshots of the owner's own posts can support a number. Crop to the title, the text, and the counters. Remove other people's content, avatars, account names, and the phone status bar. Put the capture date in the caption. If the post says something that no longer matches the page (for example a product status), say so in the caption.

## Company and employer work

- Describe the person's part at an agreed level: role, direction, scope. Do not publish internal numbers, feature names that may change, roadmaps, or internal screenshots.
- Use the employer's public pages as evidence (a marketing page screenshot with a date), and only the facts that page states.
- Feature names that might be renamed or removed are left out until the owner says otherwise. A resume can carry more detail than a public site; the two have different boundaries, so decide each one.
- Label it: "company product, source not public".
